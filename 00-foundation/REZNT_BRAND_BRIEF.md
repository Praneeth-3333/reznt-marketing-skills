# Reznt Brand & Product Brief

**Purpose:** Shared context for every marketing skill. Review against the live website before major campaigns.

## Public positioning

Reznt presents itself as an **AI Revenue Operator** for conversation-led businesses. Its central promise is that businesses should not lose opportunities because enquiries, calls, DMs, quotes, or follow-ups go unanswered.

The brand's contrast is:
- CRM: records customer information.
- Revenue intelligence: analyzes information.
- Customer-support AI: helps automate replies.
- Reznt's stated role: executes operational steps across the customer conversation and revenue journey, escalating when human judgment is needed.

Use this distinction thoughtfully. Do not attack competitors or imply every alternative lacks useful capabilities.

## Website messaging themes

- “Never miss another customer.”
- Answer, qualify, follow up, and keep selling across channels.
- One customer, one conversation, a connected history.
- Business-specific knowledge: FAQs, policies, products, catalogues, prices, and operating rules.
- Customer memory: what a customer asked, bought, objected to, and when they went quiet.
- Revenue Ledger: tracks customer momentum and the next action, not only static records.
- Governance: define what the agent can do automatically and what requires approval.
- Human handoff with conversation history and a summary.
- Conversation-led businesses are the target context.

Use exact brand phrases sparingly; write clear, audience-specific copy instead of repeating slogans everywhere.

The website presents WhatsApp and Instagram as live channels and describes calls, SMS, and email as part of its broader/multi-channel direction. Always verify current availability and scope before making a claim about a specific channel or integration.

The site also describes:
- Unified customer conversations
- AI agent reasoning, personality, memory, and escalation
- Knowledge and memory
- Governance and approvals
- Lead qualification
- Missed-call recovery
- Integrations including Google Sheets, calendar booking, Shopify/external API, as shown on pricing pages

Do not infer a feature's exact behavior from a page title alone. Confirm the relevant product page or internal product owner before publishing detailed promises.

## Voice and messaging guidance

Aim for:
- Clear, direct, commercially literate, practical
- Specific to a business problem
- Confident without unsupported guarantees
- Human-centered: automation handles routine actions; people retain judgment where needed
- Focused on missed opportunities, reply speed, qualification, follow-up consistency, customer context, and operational workload

Avoid:
- Calling Reznt merely a “chatbot” as the default description
- Promising guaranteed revenue, conversion lifts, or time savings without verified evidence
- Claiming perfect accuracy or that humans are never needed
- Suggesting the agent can make every decision autonomously
- Treating all industries or channels as equally supported
- Fabricating customer names, testimonials, case studies, statistics, or integrations

## Core messaging formula

**Audience + costly conversation problem + Reznt capability + business outcome to measure + proof/qualification.**

Example hypothesis (not a verified performance claim):
“For real estate teams handling enquiries across WhatsApp and Instagram, Reznt can help answer routine questions, qualify leads, and keep follow-ups moving. Measure the impact through response time, qualified enquiries, booked visits, and conversion.”

## Marketing proof hierarchy

1. Approved internal product documentation and verified product behavior.
2. Current official Reznt website pages and pricing.
3. Approved customer interviews, case studies, and measured results.
4. Reputable third-party sources and current competitor pages.
5. Public social posts, forums, and reviews (use as qualitative signals, not universal truth).
6. Hypotheses (label clearly and validate).
